What it simulates

Physics you can watch, measured as it runs

Black holes up close

Near a black hole, bodies move in its exact spacetime (Kerr's, when it spins), down to the innermost stable orbit. Its shadow and the sky bent around it are traced ray by ray, its edge as Synge's formula puts it. The disk here is an illustration: a thin disk as Novikov and Thorne model one.

Motion near a black hole

Gravity as Einstein wrote it

Every massive body pulls on every other in relativistic N-body gravity. Mercury's perihelion turns 42.9807″ a century, where general relativity says 42.9805″; above, the Hulse–Taylor pulsar's turns 4.2° a year.

Close pairs

Gravitational waves

Pairs spiral together as they radiate, and you can hear the chirp. GW150914 replayed merges into 63.03 Suns spinning at 0.683; LIGO measured 62 ± 4 and 0.67.

Black-hole mergers

Stars held up by quantum mechanics

White dwarfs reach Chandrasekhar's limit, 1.4563 Suns. Neutron stars top out at 2.048. Up close, a neutron star's gravity bends its light: it looks 1.25 times its size.

Neutron stars

Eclipses and the sky

The eclipse finder searches the simulation itself: all 24 eclipses of 2026 to 2030 in NASA's canon, the worst 5.0 s off. Stand anywhere on Earth and watch.

The real solar system

From NASA JPL's positions: the planets, all 459 known moons, 62 dwarf planets, asteroids and comets, and 28,331 belt objects, the planets turning as the IAU's models say. Run a year, and Earth ends within 0.41 km of JPL's. Press Now to see it as it is today.

Above: Saturn's moons at a day a second.

The Milky Way

Gaia's 21,480 stars within 50 parsecs, 251 clusters, nebulae and satellite galaxies, and 30,000 stars in balance with the galaxy's mass, each feeling what you add.

Tides and collisions

Planets become fluids on the GPU as tides take hold. Collisions leave what Leinhardt and Stewart's laws say; craters are as wide as Holsapple's give.

The scenarios

Honest about its limits

Every body says which physics moves it, and how far to trust it

Each model declares where it holds. The inspector grades every body against that, on the spot:

  • within range: the model holds here
  • approximate: higher orders start to show
  • beyond its model: said plainly, not hidden

What's an illustration rather than a measurement says so. Where physics is unknown, inside a black hole or past the published equations of state, it's labeled, not invented. The manual inside the app (F1) explains each model and where it stops being right.

Run it

Download, unzip, double-click

For Windows

One program, worldline.exe, with everything inside it. It needs Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) and a graphics card that draws through DirectX 12 or Vulkan.

Download the latest release

The program isn't signed with a paid certificate, so the first time Windows may say Windows protected your PC: press More info, then Run anyway. Before each release is published, a clean Windows machine with only the zip starts it and checks what it draws.

From source

Install Rust from rustup.rs, then:

git clone https://github.com/Bishnu2430/Worldline
cd Worldline
cargo run --release

The first build takes a few minutes. cargo test runs the checks, and cargo test --release -- --ignored the slow ones.

Some places to start

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, double-click a body to fly to it. Space runs or stops time, + and − change its speed, and F1 opens the manual. Or from a command prompt:

worldline.exe --scenario gw150914      # two black holes merge
worldline.exe --scenario hulse-taylor  # the pulsar, as a film
worldline.exe --scenario eclipse       # the eclipse of 12 August 2026
worldline.exe --quality low            # for a weaker computer
